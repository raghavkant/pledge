Research date: **1 October 2026**. Owner's goal: top 5 (realistically #3) for the "practice" and "free" searches, top 5 for the long-tail questions.

## How this was researched

- **Volumes and difficulty:** the owner's Ubersuggest export (Australia, monthly Google searches, estimates), plus Ubersuggest keyword suggestions for Australia on 1 October 2026 (same numbers where they overlap).
- **Who ranks:** Ubersuggest SERP snapshots for google.com.au (location Australia), dated 7 September to 30 September 2026. Positions count the AI Overview and "People also ask" boxes as positions, as Ubersuggest does. "DA" is Ubersuggest's domain authority (0–100).
- **What pages cover:** each competitor page downloaded and read (title, headings, structured data, claims).
- **Limits.** The SERP tool shows *that* "People also ask" appears, not its questions. Our question headings follow Australian autocomplete questions (Ubersuggest suggestions for Australia). OpenRush had no credits, so there was no second source. One search ("australian citizenship test questions") returned no Australian snapshot; its close variants ("… questions pdf", "… questions and answers") did.

### What this tells us

1. **Home Affairs owns #1–2 for the core practice searches, and should.** We don't compete with it or imitate it. The realistic prize is #3–5, which is held today by **very weak domains (DA 1–10)**. There is no strong commercial player.
2. **The long tail is thin.** "What to bring", "fail 3 times", "how many attempts" and "pass mark" are led by Facebook posts, forums, old reports and DA 3–15 blogs. Home Affairs itself is missing from several of them. A clear, sourced, answer-first page can reach the top 5.
3. **An AI Overview sits at the top of almost every results page.** Short, quotable answers with a source and a date give the best chance of being cited.
4. **"Book" means two things.** "book australian citizenship test" returns *booking* results (the test page, the appointments site) and *booklet* results (the PDF, the podcast, a council workbook). "citizenship test australia book" (1,900) leans to the booklet. So booking and the booklet each get their own page, linked to each other.
5. **"2025" searches (1,900 + 880) are last year's wording.** We don't put a past year in a title. The 2026 pages will catch these as the year turns.

## Why #3 (ozcitizenshiptest.com.au) ranks

Its domain is weak (DA 4), so links are not the reason. It ranks on:

- **An exact match:** title "Australian Citizenship Free Practice Tests 2026 Edition" and h1 "Welcome to the Australian citizenship free practice test 2026".
- **The test is on the page**, and the site is a large cluster of quiz pages (50 sets, topic tests) all linking back to the home page. That means strong internal links, and people stay and click.
- **A `.com.au` domain**, which tells Google the site is for Australia, plus some age.
- FAQPage and Organization structured data, and about 2,000 words on the home page.

What it gets wrong, and where we can be better:

- Claims we would never make: "100% pass rate", "323 Reviews", "Pass Your Test on the First Try", and "all topics and questions you will encounter in the real exam".
- No sources, no dates, no booklet page per answer.
- A heavy premium upsell, and a slow page (686 KB of HTML).

## What competitors miss (our openings)

- **Sources and dates.** Almost no competitor quotes Home Affairs word for word with a "checked on" date.
- **Invented process steps.** One booking guide (citizenshipprep) describes "log in to ImmiAccount → select your appointment → confirm". Home Affairs says it sends a letter, and you follow the letter to reschedule. Being correct is our edge.
- **The values rule explained with the page.** Most say "5/5" but don't connect each question to its page in Part 4.
- **Speed and privacy.** No ads, no sign-up, no tracking, and a fast phone page.

## Our honest weaknesses

- **The domain.** `raghavkant.github.io/pledge/` is a folder on a shared GitHub subdomain, with no Australian signal and no links yet. This is the single biggest limit on the head terms. Domain day (with a `.com.au` or `.app` domain) and a few genuine links matter more than any on-page change.
- **Free tier only.** Our questions cover Part 1 and the values part. Competitors publish all four parts; we can't and won't (rule 2). Every page says so plainly.
- **The site is new** (first pages 25–26 September 2026) and the app is not on the App Store yet.

## The practice-test page: why a redirect

The owner asked for the home page to carry the working test. Two pages with the same 20-question test would compete for the same searches. Options:

- **Canonical only:** the old page stays, with `rel=canonical` pointing to home. Google often ignores a canonical between pages that aren't duplicates, so both could still compete.
- **Merge and redirect (chosen).** GitHub Pages can't send a server 301, so the old URL becomes a small page with an instant meta refresh and a canonical link to the home page's test. Google treats an instant meta refresh as a permanent redirect. It is left out of the sitemap, and every internal link points to `/#practice-test`. The page is 5 days old, so almost nothing is lost. The question file stays at its current address.

## Structured data (valid, never promised)

- **Home:** WebSite, plus a `Quiz` describing the free test (schema.org; Google shows no special result for it).
- **Guides:** Article (author, dates) and BreadcrumbList, as now.
- **FAQPage** on pages whose visible text is questions and answers. Google now shows FAQ rich results only for well-known government and health sites, so this helps machines (including AI search) read the page; it won't change how our result looks.
- **50 questions page:** `Quiz` with `hasPart` Questions and their accepted answers, matching the visible text.
- **No HowTo** (Google retired HowTo rich results) and no ratings or reviews (we have none).

## What "done" can and can't mean

On-page work can make each page the most accurate and useful result for its search. It can't by itself move a new github.io folder past older `.com.au` sites for 18,000-a-month searches. Expected order of wins: long tail first (weeks to a few months), then "free" and "50 questions", then the practice head terms after domain day and some genuine links.
